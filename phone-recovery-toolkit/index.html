Phone Recovery Toolkit

Owner-authorized troubleshooting for iPhone and Android devices: screen-lock recovery, Apple Activation Lock, Google Factory Reset Protection (FRP), carrier/SIM unlocking, and next steps for proof-of-ownership cases.

Security boundary: this tool provides recovery instructions, not an exploit or security bypass. It cannot remove Activation Lock or FRP by itself. Use the account originally linked to the device or the manufacturer's official ownership-review process. Do not share passcodes, passwords, IMEI, or proof-of-purchase documents on this page.

Find the right recovery path

iPhone

Apple Activation Lock

  1. Use the Apple Account that originally enabled Find My, or follow the on-screen account-recovery steps.
  2. If the phone belongs to you but the account is inaccessible, recover the account through Apple.
  3. With valid proof of purchase, submit Apple's official Activation Lock support request.
  4. If bought used, ask the previous owner to remove the device from their account. Do not pay for “iCloud bypass” tools.

Android

Google FRP protection

  1. Connect to Wi-Fi and sign in with a Google Account previously synced to the phone.
  2. If you forgot the password, recover the Google Account through Google's official recovery page.
  3. After a password reset, a security waiting period may apply; follow the device's on-screen instructions.
  4. If the account cannot be recovered, contact the manufacturer or an authorized service center with proof of purchase.

Samsung

Galaxy device recovery

  1. Use the Google Account previously added to the device; a recovery-menu reset normally does not remove FRP.
  2. If you recently changed your Google password, follow the waiting period shown by Google/device guidance.
  3. If you cannot identify the account, contact an authorized Samsung service center and bring proof of purchase.
  4. Before any reset, understand that a factory reset erases local data.

Carrier

SIM / network unlock

  1. Check whether the phone says SIM/network locked rather than Activation Lock or FRP.
  2. Contact the carrier that originally sold or financed the phone and request an eligibility check.
  3. Resolve account, payment, or lost/stolen status issues through that carrier.
  4. After approval, follow the carrier's model-specific unlock instructions.

A carrier unlock is different from removing an Apple/Google account lock.

Before resetting a phone

  • A factory reset can permanently erase photos, messages, and other local data.
  • Resetting through recovery mode may trigger FRP. It does not legitimately remove the previously linked account.
  • If a phone is second-hand and still linked to another owner, ask the seller to unlink it or return it.
  • If a work/school management screen appears, contact the organization's IT administrator; only an authorized administrator can release enrollment.
  • A device IMEI/serial number alone is not proof of ownership and does not legitimately defeat account locks.
